# Changelog

Dinnerbell follows [Keep a Changelog](https://keepachangelog.com/en/1.1.0/) and [Semantic Versioning](https://semver.org/). Before version 1.0, a minor release can change configuration or behavior; its **Upgrade notes** say what to do.

Every release lists **Upgrade notes**. Read them before you upgrade, and always back up first (see "Upgrade" in [docs/setup.md](docs/setup.md)).

## [Unreleased]

### Removed

- **The upgrade guard for old feedback forms** (#148). 0.11.0 refused a **How was it?** form or modal opened before the upgrade, which still asked about the food and the place, so that saving it couldn't take back your rating. Both instances now run 0.11.0, so the guard is gone. A rating is now simply required: the web form and the Slack modal always send one, because a rating, **No answer** at first, is always selected. A web post without one gets the **Request refused** page, a Slack modal without the rating closes, and one with no rating picked says "Pick a rating, or No answer." None of them saves anything.

### Fixed

- **A History or Catalog page that isn't a number shows the first page.** An address such as `/history?page=abc` or `/catalog?q=thai&page=2.5` showed **Request refused**. It now shows the first page, and the Catalog keeps your search. A page number past either end still shows the nearest page.
- **A correction after the meal moves the count and the badges** (#162). When an admin corrects who cooked, picked up, or ordered after a meal is done, the meal's count for that role now moves to the person who has it. They earn any badge the count reaches, announced like any other and celebrated by the den. The person who lost the role loses each badge tier their counts no longer reach: a pending one is never announced or celebrated, and one already announced leaves Stats, App Home, and the recap. A tier kept through a raised count stays. Correcting it back restores both. Each correction that moves a count is audited as `event.contributions_recounted`, with the badges taken back and awarded. Before, a correction after done changed the record but not the counts.

### Upgrade notes

- **Badges:** revision `0024_badge_award_autoincrement` rebuilds the badge award table on the first start, keeping every award, so the ID of a badge taken back is never given to another. To go back to 0.11.0, restore the backup you took before the upgrade.

## [0.11.0] - 2026-10-31

### Added

- **Ratings move your own score** (#18, FR-55, [ADR 0008](docs/adr/0008-feedback-informed-ranking.md)). When you rate a meal, your score for that place or recipe moves toward your rating, from −2 for **Hated it** to +2 for **Loved it**: halfway, or three-quarters of the way for **Hated it** and **Didn't like it**. The change halves every 3 months, counts only on calls you eat at, and never vetoes anything. Logged meals count. Setting a score on **Preferences**, even back to 0, replaces your earlier ratings of it. **Preferences** shows you, and only you, what your ratings make your score, such as "From your ratings: about +1".
- **A hint for each place, from the whole household.** Once two people have rated a place, everyone sees **Well liked**, **Mixed reviews**, or **Not well liked**, without names, and recent ratings count more. The most important problem checked in the last 3 months adds to it, as in "Mixed reviews · recently late", or shows alone, as in "Recently cold". The hint is the first reason on a drawn pick, on the web and in Slack, and shows on the catalog. Words written for **Other** are never shown.
- **Feedback** on the **Ranking** tab of **Members**: how far each rating pulls a person's score, 0 (off), 1, or 2. A new household starts at 2. Hints show at every setting.
- **Adjust the plan in Slack** (#149). Once a call is decided, the coordinator taps **Adjust** on the decision message, their App Home card, or the message saying a guest with a role left, and one modal changes the meal time, who cooks, picks up, or orders (someone who's in), and who coordinates. It saves only what they changed from what the modal showed, so a change made elsewhere meanwhile, such as with **Assign roles**, is never written back, and hands off last. A refusal, such as a meal time before the decision, shows next to its field without saving anything; if a later part is refused after an earlier one saved, the reply says which. Everyone sees **Adjust** on the decision message; anyone but the coordinator who taps it is told why not, an admin included. Someone the coordinator assigned is told they can ask the coordinator to pick someone else with **Adjust**.
- **Adjust switches how you get it** (#154). When the decided place allows more than one way, the coordinator's **Adjust** modal has **How** (cook, pickup, or delivery), and its **Who does it** field assigns that person the chosen way, as **Assign roles** does: switching pickup to delivery with Alex selected makes Alex the one who orders, and they're told. Someone who can't do it that way, such as someone off a home meal's cook list, is refused next to the field. On the web, the coordinator's **Assign roles** form already switches the way at any time until the meal is done.
- **[Coordinating](docs/coordinating.md)**, a page for the household: what a coordinator is, how you become one, what you can do while answering, voting, and after the decision, in Slack and on the web, what an admin can do beyond that, and what happens when nobody coordinates or someone drops out. The README links it, with the other household guides.
- **Stuck calls show on `/healthz` and Admin** (#142). A call whose next scheduler step has been due for more than an hour, because the step keeps failing, is stuck. `/healthz` reports how many as `stuck_calls` and sets `"status": "degraded"` with HTTP 200, so the container health check doesn't restart Dinnerbell, which can't fix the step. The scheduler checks once per tick, so `/healthz` runs no extra queries. A banner on every Admin tab lists each stuck call with its meal, day, state, and how long it has been stuck, and links to the call, where you call it off (or re-run it, for a voting or decided call). A call that can't be checked counts as stuck, for an unknown time. A step that fell due while Dinnerbell was down counts from the start, so a call that catches up after a restart isn't stuck.
- **Optional: start at boot on podman** (#135). Rootless podman has no daemon, so after a reboot Dinnerbell stayed down until someone ran `podman-compose up -d`. `deploy/dinnerbell.service` and `deploy/dinnerbell-test.service` are systemd user units that run `podman-compose up -d` and `deploy/dinnerbell-test up -d` at boot and stop them at shutdown. They're in the install bundle, and "Start at boot" in docs/setup.md installs them. Rootful Docker needs neither: its daemon restarts the container. At shutdown they run `stop`, so the containers and their logs survive a reboot, and when `up` exits with an error, they try again 30 seconds later, a few times. On systemd older than 244, such as RHEL 8, add `Restart=no` in a drop-in. CI checks both units with `systemd-analyze verify`. `dinnerbell init` mentions them in one optional line at the end.
- **History and the Catalog come in pages** (#153). History shows 20 meals a page, still grouped by week, and the Catalog 20 matches a page, with **Newer** and **Older** (or **Previous** and **Next**) around "Page 2 of 5". The Catalog's page links keep your search and filters, so a page's address is a bookmark. A page past either end shows the nearest page. Logging, editing, or deleting a meal, giving feedback on it, and adding or editing a catalog item return to the page that lists it.
- **Stats has two tabs** (#153), like Admin's: **Yours**, with your badges and counts, and **Household**, at `/stats/household`, with the household's totals and newest badges. Badge posts and the monthly recap in Slack link **Household**; App Home's Stats link stays on **Yours**. Guests see only their own badges, as before, and a guest who opens **Household** lands on them.
- Documentation only: "Switch between a clone and the bundle" in docs/setup.md moves an install from a git clone to the release bundle, or back, keeping both instances' data volumes, `.env`, `.env.test`, `secrets/`, `secrets-test/`, and `compose.override.yaml`. It replaces 0.10.0's upgrade note, which said to copy `secrets/`: your account can't read the secret files, so they have to be moved.

- **Fun mode: Dinnerbone's Den** (#153, FR-62). Dinnerbone, the hamster in the logo, now lives in a den beside your greeting on **Today**: a bed of straw with his bowl and the dinner bell, and his wheel. He follows the next open call. While answers are open, he runs laps around the call's card; while it's voting, he runs in his wheel; once it's decided, he eats at his bowl; once it's done, he sleeps in the straw. He walks between them as the call changes, without a reload. With no call, he follows the clock: a nibble at breakfast, pottering about at lunch, a doze in the afternoon, waiting at his bowl at dinner, and asleep late at night, unless a call keeps him up. The den's light follows the household's time of day, and the season's weather falls in the den and down the page's edges, with snow, leaves, or a puddle that grow as the season goes on; he shakes the snow off his wheel before it turns. Tap him and he hops and a heart pops. **Celebrations:** tap **Lock this** on Today and the bell rings and he sprints to his bowl ("Pad thai it is!"); tap **Mark done** and he pats his belly and waddles to bed; earn a badge and he backflips the next time you open Today, once. Everyone else gets a quiet caption, with no animation, and so does everyone when a call is decided by the vote or in Slack. With reduced motion, he holds still and celebrations are a caption. It never makes a sound. A long name shrinks the greeting to fit beside him, then wraps. It's on by default: members turn it off with **Fun mode** on **Preferences**, and guests on their dietary-needs page, and only you change yours. With it off, pages load none of it and are exactly as before.

### Changed

- **Layout fixes, mostly for phones** (#153). Card titles on **Schedule** (Dinner, Lunch, Breakfast) and on Admin's **Ranking** and **Badges** tabs sit inside their cards instead of on the border, and those cards have room between fields. The navigation's links take two even rows on a phone instead of a ragged second row. Taps are easier: the logo, every **+** heading that opens a section, the tabs, the tag names on **Tags**, a call's title, and the catalog's meal and tag chips are at least 44 pixels tall to tap. On History, **How was it?** no longer touches the line above it.
- **Whoever coordinates is in** (#154). Tapping **I'll coordinate** changes your answer to **In** if you said Out or Maybe or hadn't answered, and so does being handed coordinating or assigned it by an admin. During the vote, you join late, as **I'm in (late)** does, but your vote counts, as a coordinator's always did; a late joiner who takes it on gets a counting vote too. A guest who coordinates is in but never late, so their dietary needs keep filtering the proposals. Someone a hand-off or an admin put in this way gets a direct message saying so. Saying **Out** ends your coordinating, as does being switched off as a late joiner, still being a maybe when answers close, or being deactivated by an admin: nobody coordinates, the call's messages say so, and anyone can tap **I'll coordinate**. Once a call is decided, only someone who's in can be handed coordinating or assigned it. A late joiner the coordinator switched off can't claim it, be handed it, or be assigned it. Each release is audited as `event.coordinator_released`. The Home reply says "You're in, and coordinating dinner."
- **Someone assigned after a hand-off is told whom to ask** (#154). When one **Adjust** both assigns a role and hands off coordinating, the assignee's message says to tell the coordinator, who can pick someone else with **Adjust**, instead of the one who just handed off.
- **Whoever loses a role is told** (#154). When the coordinator or an admin gives your cooking, pickup, or ordering to someone else before the meal time, you get "Sam changed the plan for dinner on Thursday, Oct 22: you're no longer picking up." From the meal time on, a change corrects the record of who did what, and messages nobody, the new assignee included.
- **Adjust never writes over a change saved meanwhile** (#154). Each part of an **Adjust**, the meal time, the role, and the hand-off, checks again as it saves that it's still what the modal showed, so a change saved on the web between pressing **Save** and the save itself is refused with "The plan changed since you opened this. Open Adjust again." instead of being overwritten.
- Documentation only: the spec (FR-10, FR-17, FR-58, FR-61, and sections 4, 5.7, and 10) and `docs/channels.md` describe coordinating as built (#154), including that the coordinator corrects who did what after the meal until it's marked done, which is what contributions count.
- **Admin overrides are web-only, as built** (#149). Spec 5.7 and `docs/channels.md` said an admin could assign the coordinator, move a window, re-run a call, skip a day, and correct an assignment from Slack; those are on the web app only. They now say so, and list what an admin can do in Slack: lock a pick, assign roles, and, with nobody coordinating, break a tie, shuffle, and switch late joiners.
- **The catalog shows each item's hint in place of how many loved it** (FR-49). The hint names nobody; nothing else about anyone's rating or issues is shown.
- **The issue checkboxes are in order of importance:** **Wrong order**, **Late**, **Cold**, then **Other**, on the web and in Slack. The hint picks the first one checked in that order.
- **Feedback is one rating per meal** (#18). Instead of rating the food and, for a restaurant, the place, each member gives the meal one rating: **Hated it**, **Didn't like it**, **It was fine**, **Liked it**, or **Loved it**. Every meal takes a rating, home or restaurant, and it stays optional (**No answer**). The issue chips (**Wrong order**, **Late**, **Cold**, **Other**) stay for restaurants, and **Other** gets a box to say what went wrong, up to 200 characters; typing in it checks **Other**. Only the member who wrote the text sees it, as with their rating and chips.
- **"Proposals" everywhere** (#149). Slack and the web call the places up for a vote proposals, as the web's **Proposals** heading did: the channel post is titled "Dinner proposals · Tuesday, Oct 27", **Add proposal** opens **Add a proposal**, the coordinator's button is **Shuffle proposals**, App Home links "See the proposals and vote in the channel", and the web's form adds with **Add** (or **Join late and add**), as Slack's does, instead of **Propose**. Messages and refusals that said "pick" say "proposal", such as "Pho Place is already a proposal (delivery). Vote for it instead." and "(already proposed)" beside a catalog item. "Pick up", the role, keeps its name.
- **In, Maybe, Out reads the same everywhere** (#149). The web now orders answers **In**, **Maybe**, **Out**, as Slack does, and shows them as one joined control on a row of their own, with your choice filled and checked, above the role offers, which turn on and off one by one. A guest's answers on the web get the same control. In Slack, the response reminder puts **In**, **Maybe**, and **Out** in a row of their own, apart from **View on web**, as the Your answer card and App Home already do. The web and Slack draw their button and confirmation words from one list (`domain/labels.py`): the web's roster says **Can cook**, **Can pick up**, and **Can order** instead of **Cooks**, **Picks up**, and **Orders**, which read like assignments; the buttons for a guest you answer for say **Can cook**, **Can pick up**, and **Can order** on both the web and your Slack card; **Break the tie** and **Details** have the same name on both; and **Lock this**, **Break the tie**, **Shuffle proposals**, **Mark done**, and Close now ask the same question on both.
- **App Home follows the call past the answers** (#149). While voting, Home still doesn't vote, but the call's card says which proposals you voted for, or that you haven't, and links the proposals post in the channel. With nobody coordinating, it offers **I'll coordinate**, and so does the web, as the call message already did. Once it's decided, the card names the meal time ("The plan: **Sam's Tacos** at 6:45 PM") and your own role ("You're picking up."). Home's link row adds **Stats** and **Schedule**, whether or not badge art is uploaded, and its call cards say **View on web** instead of **Open**.
- **Slack messages carry the buttons they talk about** (#149). The response reminder has the role offers and **I'll coordinate** from your Your answer card, below **In**, **Maybe**, and **Out**; a tap edits the reminder, as an answer does. A guest's invitation gets the role offers too. The "you're counted out" message after a maybe has the **I'm in (late)** button it mentions, and goes out only when the call goes on to voting: when it ends for too few people, the on-your-own post covers it. A called-off call's message, and the message to whoever started an unposted call someone else called off, have **Start dinner again** (or lunch, or breakfast), for any member until the usual meal time plus two hours. The response and feedback reminders end with a link to your preferences, where you turn reminders off. Badge posts and the monthly recap link the Stats page.
- **One name per link** (#149). Every link to an event's page says **View on web**, including the role-vacated message's (was **Pick someone**) and the call-off and on-your-own posts' (was "Open the call on the web"). Links to the Schedule page say **Schedule on the web** (was **Change the schedule** and "See the schedule"), and to the Stats page **Stats on the web**. Home's badges no longer have their own "All badges on the web" link; the link row has **Stats**.
- **An offer's buttons each do one thing** (#149). The offer message in the channel shows **Can't make it** whenever the offer is open, not only once it's full, and **Count me in** only joins: tapping it again no longer drops you out. A new **−1 guest** takes back a guest you added with **+1 guest**, in the channel and on Home, as lowering the number does on the web. The four sit in a row of their own above the item links, **View on web**, and **Call it off**.
- **`/dinnerbell cancel` calls off your offer too** (#149). Without a slot, it finds today's only open call you may call off, or your own open offer; an admin names the slot to call off someone else's offer. With a slot that has no open call you may call off, it calls off the offer there, such as yours on a day whose call you can't call off. When you may call off more than one offer in a slot, it asks you to use **Call it off** on the one you mean. The command's hint in the Slack manifest lists `schedule`, and its description mentions offering and logging.
- **Skip a day is on the Schedule page, and the admin menu is named Admin** (#149). Admins skip a day's call in a **Skip a day** section at the bottom of the Schedule page, beside the schedule it skips, instead of on the **Ranking** tab; it posts to the same address and writes the same audit row, and a bookmarked `/admin/schedule?notice=skipped` goes to the Schedule page. The navigation link to the admin pages says **Admin** instead of **Members**; its tabs are still **Members**, **Ranking**, and **Badges**.

The time zone is `DINNERBELL_TIMEZONE`, read on first start, and the last backup's time is in `/healthz`. The setup and troubleshooting guides say **Admin** > **Members** where they said **Members**.

Each line has the traceback, the event ID, the failing `step`, and `failures`, the number of failures since the previous line.

- Documentation only: shorter, easier-to-scan user docs (#137). `docs/setup.md` keeps the first install, going live, backups, upgrades, and the optional setup, at about a third of its length. ### Upgrade notes

- **Feedback:** revision `0020_one_rating` converts the feedback members already gave, on the first start. It scores each old answer (loved it +2, fine 0, not again −2) and gives the meal a rating from that score; when someone rated both the food and the place, it uses the average. So food loved it becomes **Loved it**, food loved it with place fine becomes **Liked it**, and food fine with place not again becomes **Didn't like it**. Issue chips, thank-yous, and notes to the cook stay as they were. To go back to 0.10.0, restore the backup you took before the upgrade.
- **Ratings and ranking:** revision `0021_feedback_ranking` sets **Feedback** to 0 for your household on the first start, so ratings don't move anyone's score yet, and the household hints show at once. It also records when each member last set a score for each place, so a score set before the upgrade still wins over earlier ratings. When you're ready to let ratings move scores, set **Feedback** to 2 on the **Ranking** tab of **Members**. The loved counts on the catalog are gone; the hints replace them.
- **Fun mode:** revision `0022_fun_mode` adds the fun mode setting, on for every member and guest, and `0023_fun_seen_award` records each member's newest badge so far, so the den doesn't backflip for badges earned before the upgrade. Both run on the first start. To go back to 0.10.0, restore the backup you took before the upgrade.
- `/healthz` has a new key, `stuck_calls`: a number, or `null` when the check failed or is more than 90 seconds old (the call tick hung while the heartbeat goes on). Either `null` case also reports `degraded`. No database revision ships for this.
- **Monitoring:** the documented monitors don't alert on `degraded`, which keeps HTTP 200. To be alerted about stuck calls, and about Slack, the outbox, and backups, replace the `"slack":"connected"` keyword monitor with one for `"status":"ok","version"` (see "Monitor it" in [docs/hardening.md](docs/hardening.md#monitor-it)).
- **Start at boot:** nothing to do; an install without the unit runs as before.
- **Coordinating:** nothing to do. Calls already under way keep their coordinator, even one who isn't in; the new rule applies from their next change. An **Adjust** modal open across the upgrade still saves, without **How**.
- **Slack (optional):** the `/dinnerbell` command's hint and description changed in `deploy/slack-app-manifest.yaml` (and the test manifest). Slack shows the old ones until you paste the new manifest into your app's **App Manifest** page; everything works either way. No new scopes or events. Offer and call messages already in the channel get their new buttons the next time they change.
- **History bookmarks:** History's `?page=` used to count 8-week windows from 0; it now counts 20-meal pages from 1. An old `/history?page=N` bookmark opens a different set of meals, and `?page=0` opens the newest page.

## [0.10.0] - 2026-10-29

### Added

- **Install without cloning** (#33). Each release attaches `dinnerbell-X.Y.Z.tar.gz` and its SHA-256: `compose.yaml` pinned to the release, `compose.secrets.yaml`, `.env.example`, both Slack manifests, `deploy/dinnerbell-test` with its compose files, and `INSTALL.md`. CI builds it from the tag and attests it, so `gh attestation verify` covers it like the image. [docs/install.md](docs/install.md) installs from it in eight steps.
- **`dinnerbell init`** writes a new env file. It asks for the hostname, admin email, time zone, household name, and the Slack app's values, with a hint for each; generates `DINNERBELL_SECRET_KEY`; and checks the result as `dinnerbell config check` does. Secrets are read without echo and never printed. It writes to `--output FILE` with mode 600, or to standard output when that isn't a terminal. `init --test` writes a test instance's file and refuses production's secrets, client ID, and hostname from `.env`.

### Changed

- Documentation only: step 9 of the setup guide again explains that `DINNERBELL_BASE_URL` must match the public URL, what `DINNERBELL_ADMIN_EMAIL` does, that `DINNERBELL_HOUSEHOLD_NAME` and `DINNERBELL_TIMEZONE` apply on first start only, and which secret keys Dinnerbell refuses. 0.9.1's rewrite had dropped these notes.
- **Badges climb three ladders** (#130). Frequent (1, 10, 50, 100, 200) keeps the meal badges; Occasional (1, 5, 20, 50, 100) is for Bright Idea and Good Host; Rare (1, 3, 10, 25, 50) is for On a Roll, Scout, and Explorer, so every Diamond is within reach. Stats and App Home show each badge's next step on its own ladder.
- **On a Roll has a grace week.** A run survives one missed week, which doesn't count toward it; two missed weeks in a row still end it.
- **Admin > Badges edits the three ladders**, five counts each, validated as before, with **Reset to defaults**. Each save and reset is audited. The `badge_thresholds` setting holds one list per ladder; a single list from an earlier version is read as the Frequent ladder.
- Tiers that the new ladders or the grace week put in reach are awarded quietly, as when an admin lowers a threshold, so the channel gets no burst of posts.
- Documentation only: the README opens with what Dinnerbell needs (a container host, a Slack workspace, and an HTTPS hostname), a diagram of how the pieces connect, and how a dinner works in five steps (#34).

### Upgrade notes

- **Badges:** on the first start, members may receive badge tiers quietly: the new ladders and On a Roll's grace week put them in reach. Nothing is posted to Slack for them, and no earned tier is removed. A custom ladder you saved earlier becomes the Frequent ladder. No database revision ships for this.
- **Install bundle:** optional. An install from a git clone keeps working, and upgrades the same way. To move to the bundle, see [docs/install.md](docs/install.md); keep the directory name `dinnerbell` so compose finds the same data volume, and copy `.env` (and `.env.test`, `secrets/`, `secrets-test/` if you use them) into it.

## [0.9.1] - 2026-10-29

### Fixed

- **An upgrade to 0.9.0 with the secrets still in `.env` didn't start.** 0.9.0's `compose.yaml` mounted four secret files unconditionally, so podman-compose stopped with `statfs .../secrets/DINNERBELL_SECRET_KEY: no such file or directory` when they didn't exist. Secret files are now optional. `compose.yaml` reads the secrets from `.env` again, as in 0.8.0, and keeps 0.9.0's resource limits, log rotation, and other settings. The new `compose.secrets.yaml` mounts the files when you add it: `podman-compose -f compose.yaml -f compose.secrets.yaml up -d`. `deploy/dinnerbell-test` adds it, pointed at `secrets-test/` (by the new `deploy/compose.test-secrets.yaml`), when any of the four files is there, and then requires all four; otherwise test reads its secrets from `.env.test`. Its shared-secret check compares production and test however each holds them. A secret in an env file stays supported: Dinnerbell logs a warning for each one at start, and the warning no longer says a later release ignores it. `.env.example` and `.env.test.example` list the four secrets again, and "Keep secrets in files" in docs/setup.md is now an optional section. The placeholder-key check from 0.9.0 is unchanged.

### Changed

- Documentation only: the setup guide no longer has a "Set the trusted proxy" step, its `.env` template line, or the "Rate limiting blocks everyone at once" troubleshooting row. `DINNERBELL_TRUSTED_PROXIES` stays an optional setting, described in the configuration reference and `.env.example`: set one proxy address, never a range, and never an address that other clients share, such as a container gateway. Behavior and defaults are unchanged.

### Added

- `.github/CODEOWNERS` assigns the owner to CI, the Containerfile, dependency locks, `compose.yaml`, `compose.secrets.yaml`, `deploy/`, and `scripts/`.

The backend never reaches the final image.

- The Slack manifests no longer request the unused `channels:history` scope, so a stolen bot token can't read channel history.
- `compose.yaml` (and so the test instance) now sets `pids_limit: 256`, `mem_limit: 512m`, and rotated `json-file` logs (10 MB, 3 files), and `scripts/check-compose.sh` checks them. See "Resource and log limits" in docs/setup.md for what each podman-compose version honors.
- A test now compares the vendored htmx file with the SHA-256 of the npm `htmx.org@2.0.11` release, so a modified file fails CI. `CONTRIBUTING.md` says how to update it.
- The promotion checklist and "Verify an image" now verify the attestation against the pulled image's digest, and docs/setup.md and docs/hardening.md say to pin production to that digest or the version tag, and that the test instance's `main` tag moves.
- The four secrets (`DINNERBELL_SECRET_KEY`, `DINNERBELL_OIDC_CLIENT_SECRET`, `DINNERBELL_SLACK_BOT_TOKEN`, and `DINNERBELL_SLACK_APP_TOKEN`) are now files in `secrets/` (test: `secrets-test/`) that compose mounts at `/run/secrets`, and no longer lines in `.env`. podman-compose passed every `.env` line to podman as `-e NAME=value`, so while podman created the container, each secret was on its command line, visible to other local users in `ps` and printed by `podman-compose --dry-run` and `--verbose` (#35). Dinnerbell, `deploy/dinnerbell-test`, its shared-secret check, and `dinnerbell config check` (new option `--secrets-dir`) all read the files. A missing, unreadable, directory, multi-line, or oversized (over 8 KiB) secret file, or a broken symlink, stops startup with a message that names the file. The new `deploy/check-secrets` checks the four files before `up` and prints no value. `secrets/` and `secrets-test/` are git-ignored, left out of the image build context, and `scripts/check-compose.sh` (run in CI) fails when git tracks anything there except the `*.example` placeholders.

Calls already counted keep their counts.

Open the Dinnerbell app's Home tab to join, or ask the admin."), instead of the web app's address, the version, and the database revision (audit #14). ### Fixed

- A Slack or internet outage no longer drops queued notifications (#14). An unavailable channel used to burn all 8 attempts in about an hour, and every pending row failed for good. Now an outage doesn't count as an attempt: rows retry at least every 5 minutes and are delivered, in order, when Slack returns. A row older than 24 hours is marked failed with a logged reason instead of being sent late. A response reminder queued before the call went to a vote, was decided, or finished is also dropped unsent instead of arriving late. The 24-hour limit counts from the newest update merged into a row. It is final: after 24 hours a call-opened, proposals, decision, badge, or recap post is lost for good (badges are marked announced and the recap marker moves when the post is queued), and you see `notification expired` in the log and `outbox.failed` in `/healthz`. `/healthz` now also reports `outbox.oldest_pending_age` (seconds) and shows `degraded` when the oldest waiting notification is over an hour old. Slack auth errors that retrying can't fix (such as `missing_scope`) now fail the notification at once. A queued role-choosing DM and a maybe-resolved DM are also dropped when the call moved on or was called off. FR-34 and `docs/channels.md` now say what is guaranteed: at least once after a crash, with a duplicate possible after the 5-minute lease.
- A redelivered or double-tapped **Vote**, **I can cook**, **I can pick up**, **I'll order**, or **Count me in** button no longer undoes itself (audit #14). Dinnerbell ignores a button press whose Slack `trigger_id` it handled in the last 10 minutes (it remembers up to 2,048), which is how Slack repeats a press after a reconnect. It also ignores a second press of the same toggle by the same person within 2 seconds, and says so privately ("Got it, that's already counted. Tap again in a moment to undo."). A press minutes later still flips it, as before. Redelivered modal submissions (**Make an offer**, **Log a meal**, and the other forms) are ignored the same way, so they can't post a second offer or log a second meal. If handling a press or submission fails, Dinnerbell forgets it, so a retry goes through.
- A reminder or other direct message queued before a member was deactivated is no longer sent to their Slack account: Dinnerbell drops it with a log line (a warning for a role message, and a count in memory). Deactivated members also show as plain names, not `@` mentions, in posts (audit #14).
- Slack refusing a post with `invalid_arguments` for a reason that has nothing to do with badge art no longer erases the cached art files. Dinnerbell now forgets them only when the text-only retry goes through, and a failure while forgetting them can no longer make the outbox post the badge twice (audit #14).
- `DINNERBELL_SECRET_KEY` now refuses the placeholder from `secrets/DINNERBELL_SECRET_KEY.example`, any value that contains `replace`, `changeme`, `placeholder`, `example`, `yoursecret`, or `secretkeyhere`, and any value with fewer than 8 different characters. The error names the variable and never shows the value. The example file now holds `REPLACE-ME-with-48-random-characters`, and `deploy/check-secrets` flags a placeholder, short, or repetitive key before you start.
- Two processes migrating one database at once no longer crash the second with `database is locked`. Migrations start with `BEGIN IMMEDIATE`, so the second waits and finds the schema current. If the first holds the lock for about 15 seconds, `migrate` and `serve` print a clear message and exit with status 1.
- The nightly cleanup of unfinished backups now also removes `pre-*.partial` files (from `reset-history`) once they are over an hour old.
- A catalog file with a date that doesn't exist (`name: 2001-13-45`) or a number of thousands of digits no longer ends in a 500 page. The import shows a readable error.
- Docs (#99): the ranking page, the spec, and ADR 0007 no longer say a place in the vote is always drawn. The last place is only not reserved for a suggestion, so at temperature 0 it can be one. The 0.7.0 entry below still has the old wording.

- **A `DINNERBELL_SECRET_KEY` with a placeholder marker makes the service refuse to start, and under `restart: unless-stopped` it crash-loops.** Run `deploy/check-secrets` before `up`. The refused keys are the text in `secrets/DINNERBELL_SECRET_KEY.example`, any key containing `example`, `replace`, `changeme`, `placeholder`, `yoursecret`, or `secretkeyhere` (case, `-`, and `_` ignored), and any key with fewer than 8 distinct characters. Keep your old key if it is long and random: marker words and fewer than 8 distinct characters are the only new triggers. If you do rotate the key, that invalidates only open login pages and import previews, not sessions (sessions are random server-side tokens).
- **Hard stop: create four secret files before you run `up` on this release.** The new `compose.yaml` mounts `secrets/DINNERBELL_SECRET_KEY`, `secrets/DINNERBELL_OIDC_CLIENT_SECRET`, `secrets/DINNERBELL_SLACK_BOT_TOKEN`, and `secrets/DINNERBELL_SLACK_APP_TOKEN`. If you pull and run `up` first, the service won't start. What happens with a missing file depends on the tool: podman-compose 1.0.6 and 1.6.0 pass the path to podman as a `--volume` without checking it (confirmed in their source), and podman refuses a bind mount whose source is missing (documented, not run here). Docker creates a directory for a missing `-v` source; what Docker Compose does for a missing secret file is not verified. A directory in place of a file makes Dinnerbell stop with an "is a directory" message. Run `deploy/check-secrets` (new) first: it reports a missing file, a directory, a mode other than 600, and an empty file (except an empty Slack token file, which turns Slack off), and prints no value. Dinnerbell itself still accepts a secret from `.env` for this one release, with a warning for each; the next release ignores `.env` for these four. If a value is in both `.env` and its file with different values, Dinnerbell refuses to start. Run this from the clone, with the service still running:

  ```sh
  ( umask 077
    mkdir -p secrets
    for name in SECRET_KEY OIDC_CLIENT_SECRET SLACK_BOT_TOKEN SLACK_APP_TOKEN; do
        sed -n "s/^[[:space:]]*\(export[[:space:]]\{1,\}\)\{0,1\}DINNERBELL_$name[[:space:]]*=[[:space:]]*//p" .env | tail -n 1 | tr -d '\r' | sed -e 's/[[:space:]]*$//' -e 's/^"\(.*\)"$/\1/' -e "s/^'\(.*\)'\$/\1/" | tr -d '\n' >"secrets/DINNERBELL_$name"
    done )
  deploy/check-secrets --env-file .env      # every secret must say "match"
  for name in SECRET_KEY OIDC_CLIENT_SECRET SLACK_BOT_TOKEN SLACK_APP_TOKEN; do
      chmod 600 "secrets/DINNERBELL_$name"
      podman unshare chown 10001:10001 "secrets/DINNERBELL_$name"
  done
  ```

  The `sed` reads each value from `.env` without putting it on a command line, removes one pair of enclosing quotes and nothing else, and leaves no trailing newline. `deploy/check-secrets --env-file .env` compares SHA-256 hashes and prints only `match` or `differs`; run it before the `chown`, because afterward your account can't read the files. If a value has a trailing `# comment` or an escape in double quotes, write that file by hand: `printf '%s' 'the value' >secrets/DINNERBELL_SECRET_KEY` (put a space before the command to keep it out of your shell history), then `chmod 600` and `chown` it. An empty file is fine for a Slack token you don't use, but the file must exist. Then delete the four lines from `.env`, pull the new image, and run `podman-compose down && podman-compose up -d`. Check that the log has no "is set in the environment" warning.
- With rootful Docker, replace `podman unshare chown 10001:10001` with `sudo chown 10001:10001`. The files must belong to UID 10001, the user inside the container, with mode 600: compose bind-mounts them as they are and ignores `uid`, `gid`, and `mode` for file secrets, so a file owned by your own account is unreadable inside the container. Your own account can no longer read the files afterward; to replace one, delete it, write it again, and `chown` it again.
- If Dinnerbell stops at start with `Can't read the secret file`, the owner or mode is wrong: run the `chmod` and `chown` again. If it says the path `is a directory`, the file didn't exist when the engine mounted it: remove the directory, create the file, and recreate the service. The table in "Keep secrets in files" in [docs/setup.md](docs/setup.md#keep-secrets-in-files) lists each message.
- **Test instance:** do the same for `.env.test`, into `secrets-test/` instead of `secrets/` (the `sed` reads `.env.test`). The new `deploy/dinnerbell-test` refuses to start without the four files in `secrets-test/`, and, as before, refuses a secret that test shares with production, now in files as well as in `.env` and `.env.test`. It compares SHA-256 hashes, and reads files owned by UID 10001 through `podman unshare` (with Docker, run it with `sudo`).
- Food Critic upgrade: no migration, settings, or Slack scope changes. Food Critic awards made before the upgrade stay out of the recap and the newest badges; ones earned after it appear in both. No Food Critic tier is ever announced.
- The next prewarm uploads 5 more files, one per Food Critic tier, two seconds apart.
- **Resource and log limits.** `compose.yaml` now sets `pids_limit: 256`, `mem_limit: 512m`, and `json-file` log rotation (10 MB, 3 files). They apply when you recreate the service with `podman-compose down && podman-compose up -d`. If the service is close to 512 MB on your host, check `podman stats` first.
- **`/healthz` changes.** It gains `backup.failing` and `outbox.oldest_pending_age` (seconds), and reports `degraded` when the newest nightly backup failed or the oldest pending notification is over 1 hour old. A monitor that alerts on anything other than `ok` can fire where it didn't before.
- **Gambler is not backfilled.** Calls already counted keep their counts, so a call that now earns Gambler under the new rule (a vote for none with no other voters) gives nobody the badge retroactively. It applies to calls that finish after the upgrade.

- Proposals are drawn at random, weighted by score, instead of the top scores with ties broken by name (#78).   - Places with the same score have the same chance, whatever their names. A place 1 point better is about 2.7 times as likely to be picked, so the best place usually makes the vote but not every time.
  - A place someone suggested for the call is always proposed when it fits, ahead of the draw.
  - After each pick, places sharing its cuisine become less likely, so a vote rarely has three of one cuisine when others fit.
  - The same call always draws the same picks. A re-run, or starting a called-off call again, draws again. The order drawn is the proposals' rank, which still breaks a tied vote when neither the coordinator nor an admin voted for a tied place.
  - **Admin > Ranking** has a new **Randomness** setting, from 0 to 3, default 1. Lower sticks closer to the best scores, and 0 always picks them in order, with ties drawn at random. Higher mixes the picks up more. Changing it is audited with the other ranking settings.

While responses are open, the call message has one **Answer** button instead of **In**, **Maybe**, **Out**, the role offers, **I'll coordinate**, and **Details**. **Answer** posts a Your answer card only you see, with those buttons and your answer and offers selected: green, and marked "✓ " so you can tell without color. Taps on the card change the card in place instead of adding another message. Tapping **Answer** again adds a new card, because Slack can't change one person's view of a shared message. The card replaces the "Thanks! You're offering to cook" lines, which piled up. While voting with nobody coordinating, the call message still has **I'll coordinate**.
- The reminder direct message and App Home show your answer selected the same way. A tap on the reminder changes the reminder itself, instead of adding a confirmation under it.
- A card or reminder left over from before a call moved on (responses closed, called off, or started again) says "This call moved on." instead of answering the newer call.

### Fixed

- Starting a call for a later day in Slack no longer says "Started … It's posted in the Dinnerbell channel." when nothing is posted until that day (#59). The reply says "Scheduled the lunch call for Sunday, Oct 25. It posts in #dinnerbell at 10:30 AM that day." A scheduled call's web card says when it posts, and which day, instead of "Opens at 10:30 AM."
- A call scheduled for a later day no longer hides **Start a dinner call** (or lunch) for today on App Home.
- **I'll coordinate** on a voting call message names the call's run, so a message from before a call-off and restart can't make you coordinator of the new run (#54).
- **Start the vote now** and **Decide now** no longer show once the window's deadline has passed. Until the scheduler's next tick moved the call on, up to 30 seconds later, the web card and App Home still offered them, and selecting one only said the call had moved on.
- In Slack, picking **Start the vote now** or **Decide now** from the call's ⋯ menu after the deadline says the call has moved on right away, instead of opening a confirmation that could only fail.
- Tapping **I'll coordinate** again when you already coordinate no longer says someone else is coordinating.
- Details saved in Slack after the call was called off and started again no longer change the new call.

### Upgrade notes

- The audit log names schedule changes differently. Ranking changes on Admin > Ranking are now `household.ranking_updated`, and a member's change to a slot's schedule is `household.slot_schedule_updated`. Rows written before you upgrade keep `household.schedule_updated`. If you search the audit log for schedule changes, search for all three.
- The database migrates to revision `0008_meal_feedback` when Dinnerbell starts, in two steps: `0007_event_autoincrement` rebuilds the `event` table so call IDs are never reused, keeping every row, and `0008_meal_feedback` adds an empty `feedback` table. Both are quick for a household's data. Back up first, as always. To go back to an earlier release, restore that backup: older releases refuse a database at a revision they don't know.
- Decision messages for calls that finished before you upgrade don't get **How was it?** until they're redrawn. Those meals still take feedback from App Home and the web.
- Members who already turned on the feedback reminder in Preferences, which did nothing until now, start getting it for meals that finish after the upgrade, unless they muted direct-message reminders.
- Optional: the Slack app manifests list `version` in the slash command's usage hint. To show it in Slack, open the app's **Slash Commands** settings and edit the command's usage hint to match `deploy/slack-app-manifest.yaml` (or `deploy/slack-app-manifest.test.yaml` for test). `/dinnerbell version` works without this.
- Optional, for test: the test Slack app's manifest lists `discard` in the slash command's usage hint. To show it, edit the `/dinnerbell-test` command's usage hint to match `deploy/slack-app-manifest.test.yaml`.
- Call messages and reminders already posted before you upgrade keep their old buttons until they're redrawn. They still work: the call's old buttons post your card, and an old reminder's buttons reply with a line of text.

Exports are sorted, so two exports of the same catalog are identical, and importing one back changes nothing. They never include cooks, members, preferences, history, or anything else personal. To copy the catalog between production and test, see [docs/setup.md](docs/setup.md#copy-the-catalog-between-production-and-test).

A missing setting, such as `DINNERBELL_ADMIN_EMAIL`, now stops it with the setting's name, instead of starting a container that restarts forever. `DINNERBELL_ENGINE` picks the container engine for the check.

It never prints the values.
- [docs/setup.md](docs/setup.md#test-instance) shows how both instances share one clone, how to write `.env.test` from `.env` without copying production's secrets, and what to do when the test port hangs.
- Calling a call off isn't admin-only anymore (FR-56). The coordinator can call off a call at any point, and whoever started an ad-hoc call can until it's decided. An optional reason of up to 140 characters goes with it.
  - The reply in the call's thread names who called it off and why, and mentions everyone who was in or maybe and the coordinator. The call message says "Called off by" with the reason, and a decision is struck through.
  - When a decided call is called off, each member assigned to cook, pick up, or order gets a direct message, and assignment or reminder messages that haven't gone out yet are dropped.
  - A call that was never posted, such as one scheduled for a later day, posts nothing. Its starter gets a direct message if someone else called it off.
- Responses close on their own once every active member has answered and nobody is a maybe. If everyone is out, the call ends on-your-own at the next scheduler tick. Otherwise it waits until no response has changed for 10 minutes, so you can add guests, an eat window, or an offer after tapping **In**; with two or more eating, someone must also be able to cook, pick up, or order. Voting then gets its usual length. Answers kept from before a re-run or restart don't count.
- Starting a call for a slot and day that was called off starts it again, for any member, as long as it isn't past the usual meal time plus two hours. It starts with no coordinator.
- On the web, a call's card on Today and on its own page shows **Call it off** to whoever may call it off, and the coordinator and admins get **Start the vote now** while responses are open and **Decide now** while voting. **Call it off** opens a small form with an optional reason and asks you to confirm. A called-off call reads "Called off by {name}" with the reason, and anyone can select **Start dinner again** (or lunch) until the usual meal time plus two hours. Every post is checked again on the server, and a refusal shows on the card in plain words. **Start the vote now** and **Decide now** change nothing if the call has moved on, or if someone else closed the window first, and a card left open while someone called the call off and started it again can't act on the new run; either way, the card asks you to reload.
- Admins can skip one day's call, today or up to 7 days ahead, before it goes out, from **Skip a day** on Admin > Schedule. It offers only meals that have a daily call, and refuses a meal that's off in the schedule. Nothing is posted, the skip is audited, and anyone can still start that call on the day.
- Call it off and Close now in Slack (FR-56):
  - While a window is open, the call message has a ⋯ menu with **Call it off** and **Start the vote now** or **Decide now**. Once decided, the call and decision messages have a **Call it off** button. Anyone who isn't allowed gets a private reply saying who can.
  - **Call it off** opens a modal with an optional reason. Close now asks you to confirm, and changes nothing if the call has moved on by then, or if someone else closed the window first. A menu, button, or modal left over from before a call was re-run or started again does nothing.
  - App Home shows **Call it off** and Close now only to people who can use them, and **Start dinner again** or **Start lunch again** after a call-off, until it's too late.
  - `/dinnerbell cancel [lunch|dinner] [day] [reason]` calls off a call. Without a slot, it calls off that day's only open call, and asks which one when lunch and dinner are both open.
  - A called-off decision message is struck through and says who called it off and why.

### Changed

- The event page's admin-only **Cancel this call** and **Close now** buttons are gone: **Call it off**, **Start the vote now**, and **Decide now** replace them for admins and members alike. For a called-off call, admins see **Start dinner again** instead of **Re-run**. The `POST /events/{id}/admin/cancel` route is removed; use `POST /events/{id}/cancel`.

## [0.2.0] - 2026-10-23

### Added

- Prebuilt container images on GitHub Container Registry at `ghcr.io/dsmorgan/dinnerbell`. Each release publishes `X.Y.Z`, `X.Y`, and `latest`, and every push to `main` publishes `main` and the short commit SHA. Images carry build provenance and SBOM attestations; [docs/setup.md](docs/setup.md#verify-an-image) shows how to verify one.
- The footer and `/healthz` show the running version, such as `0.2.0`, or `0.2.0+abc1234` for a build from `main`.
- Test instance support ([ADR 0006](docs/adr/0006-test-instance.md)): run a second copy with its own database, Slack app, and hostname.
  - `DINNERBELL_INSTANCE_LABEL` shows a marker such as `TEST` on the web, in Slack posts, on App Home, and in `/healthz`. While it's set, Dinnerbell mentions and messages only members of the configured channel.
  - `DINNERBELL_SLACK_COMMAND` names the slash command. Default: `/dinnerbell`.
  - `DINNERBELL_FEATURES` turns on feature flags. Unknown names stop startup.
  - `dinnerbell scrub-test --keep EMAIL [--keep EMAIL ...] [--yes]` makes a restored production backup safe to use in test. It shows what it will change and asks you to confirm.
  - `deploy/dinnerbell-test` runs every test command with the test project, volume, env file, tag, and port, and refuses unsafe combinations. `deploy/slack-app-manifest.test.yaml` and `.env.test.example` set up the rest.
- `compose.yaml` reads `DINNERBELL_TAG`, `DINNERBELL_ENV_FILE`, and `DINNERBELL_PORT`, which only `deploy/dinnerbell-test` sets. Never set them in `.env` or `export` them: an exported `DINNERBELL_ENV_FILE=.env.test` makes production's own compose commands run with test's settings on production's database.

### Changed

- `compose.yaml` pulls the release image instead of building from source. To build from source, add `-f compose.build.yaml`.
- Production's clone follows release tags: to upgrade, check out the new `vX.Y.Z` tag instead of running `git pull` on `main`.

### Upgrade notes

You switch from building the image to pulling it. Follow "Switch a source-built install to the prebuilt image" in [docs/setup.md](docs/setup.md), which also has undo steps. In short:

1. Note the commit you run now, and back up the database.
2. Run `git fetch --tags && git checkout v0.2.0`, then `podman-compose pull`.
3. Recreate the service: `podman-compose down && podman-compose up -d`.

Run these as the same user and from the same directory as your install. Your data volume keeps its name, so the database carries over. If your install predates M2, its migrations run on start.

podman-compose 1.0.6 or later is required.
